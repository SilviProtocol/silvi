## Key Findings at a Glance

### What the Industry Leaders Do (2025-2026)

1. **NASA/ESA**: Uncertainty quantification (gold standard), ensemble methods, operational forecasting
2. **eBird (Cornell)**: Weekly temporal predictions, 2,974 species, GAM ensembles, validated
3. **Map of Life (Yale)**: 1km global coverage, 46,000 tree species, multi-source integration
4. **NatureServe**: Human-AI collaboration, expert validation, rare species focus
5. **BioDT (EU)**: Digital twin forecasting, scenario planning, DestinE integration
6. **Microsoft**: SPARROW edge computing, Planetary Computer STAC catalog
7. **Google**: Earth Engine SDM framework, SpeciesNet (image ID)
8. **GBIF**: 1.6B+ occurrences, DNA-derived data, cloud access (AWS/GCS/Azure)

### What Treekipedia Does Better

1. **10m resolution** (3-100× finer than all competitors)
2. **Tree species specialization** (48,129 species with geohashes)
3. **Blockchain verification** (EAS attestations + NFTs - nobody else has this)
4. **Native status integration** (WCVP 99.99% coverage)
5. **Community incentive model** (crypto rewards for contributions)

### Critical Gaps vs. Industry

1. ❌ **Uncertainty quantification** (now mandatory in 2025+)
2. ❌ **Ensemble methods** (Random Forest + BART + MaxEnt)
3. ❌ **Validation framework** (TSS, AUC, independent test sets)
4. ❌ **Explainable AI** (SHAP for interpretability)
5. ❌ **Polygon/AOI support** (currently point-only)
6. ❌ **Temporal forecasting** (static predictions only)

| **Ensemble** | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ Gold standard | Meta-analysis | Reduces method-choice uncertainty |
| **MaxEnt** | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ Competitive | Standard baseline | Still widely used, not always best |
